A: Uncle Wei Lun, I am Xiao Chuan. I've been listening to your mother's stories. Today I'd like to hear yours.
K: Mine? I don't have any stories lah. My mother's, that one is a story.
A: You also grew up at Jalan Bandar, right?
K: Yes. By the time I was born the shop was already closed. We lived upstairs, whole floor, and there was an airwell at the back.
A: What was upstairs like?
K: Wooden staircase, step on it and it makes noise. When I was small I go down in the middle of the night, step on the third step and it always goes "krrk" — then my mother knows I'm awake.
A: Haha.
K: Downstairs the shop was empty, my father used it to put things. Me and my friends cycled inside, going round and round. Floor is cement, very loud when you cycle.
A: How old were you then?
K: Seven, eight maybe. Mid-seventies.
A: And your father?
K: My father drove a lorry. Three, four days a week he's not home — running to KL, running to Johor Bahru. Come back and sleep, wake up and go.
A: Did you wait for him to come back?
K: Yes lah. Every time he came back he brought something. Not anything good, just whatever he bought on the road. One time he brought a packet of dried mango, sour like anything, we few still fought over it.
A: You still remember the taste?
K: Remember. Later in KL I saw them selling it, bought a packet. Not correct. Not that taste anymore.
A: ……
K: Talk this for what. What do you want to ask?
A: Nothing, I'm listening.
K: Mm. What did my mother tell you?
A: She told me a lot. She says you are very busy.
K: …I am very busy.
A: Uncle Wei Lun, shall we stop here today?
K: Okay.
