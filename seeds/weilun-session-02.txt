A: Uncle Wei Lun, last time you talked about the dried mango. What would you like to talk about today?
K: You still remember ah. Today let's talk about moving to KL, it's the same thing anyway.
A: Alright.
K: Ninety-three, or ninety-four. I couldn't find work in Ipoh, that time Ipoh had no work. One of my classmates was doing engineering in KL, he called me down.
A: What did your mother say?
K: She didn't say anything. Just packed my things, packed for three days. One travel bag, she stuffed it until it couldn't close.
A: What did she put in?
K: Clothes, biscuits, a tin of coffee powder. And a kettle for boiling water, she insisted I bring it. I said KL also sells kettles, she said buying costs money.
A: Do you still have that kettle?
K: Spoiled long ago. But I used it more than ten years.
A: What was it like when you got to KL?
K: Stayed in Cheras, one small room. Very noisy at night, traffic. First week I couldn't sleep — not because of the noise, because it was too quiet. I mean, nobody going up and down the stairs.
A: Mm.
K: That time phoning home was expensive, call once a week, cannot even talk five minutes. My mother every time asked whether I had eaten, and every time I said I ate.
A: Did you eat?
K: Sometimes no.
A: ……
K: Now when I think about it, should have called more. That time I thought, she's there anyway, no hurry.
A: Uncle Wei Lun —
K: It's fine. I know what you want to say.
A: I haven't said anything yet.
K: Ha. Okay lah, that's enough for today.
